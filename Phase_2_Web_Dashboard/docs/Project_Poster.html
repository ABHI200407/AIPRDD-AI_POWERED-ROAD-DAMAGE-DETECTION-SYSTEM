📄 Abstract

Road infrastructure degradation poses a critical safety and economic challenge across developing and developed nations alike. Traditional manual inspection methods are time-consuming, inconsistent, and costly, leading to delayed maintenance and elevated accident risk. This paper presents an AI-Powered Road Damage Detection System that leverages the YOLOv8 Medium object detection architecture to automatically classify pavement defects from citizen-submitted images in real time. The system is trained on the RDD2022 benchmark dataset, comprising road images from India and Japan, across four internationally standardized damage categories: longitudinal cracking (D00), transverse cracking (D10), alligator cracking (D20), and potholes (D40). The full-stack web application integrates a FastAPI backend, a React + Vite.js frontend, and a SQLite database to enable citizens to report road damage, track repair status, and visualize community reports on an interactive map. The trained model achieves 94.5% accuracy after 100 epochs of training on 1,530 annotated images using the SGD optimizer. The system provides an automated severity-scoring mechanism and an administrative dashboard for municipal authorities to prioritize and manage repair workflows efficiently, bridging the gap between AI research and practical smart-city infrastructure management.

🔍 Damage Classification Categories
D00
Longitudinal Crack
Cracks parallel to road center-line; often from lane-edge fatigue or pavement shrinkage.
D10
Transverse Crack
Cracks perpendicular to road axis; typically thermal expansion/contraction or reflective cracking.
D20
Alligator Crack
Interconnected fatigue cracks forming a pattern resembling alligator skin — severe structural distress.
D40
Pothole
Bowl-shaped depressions caused by water infiltration and traffic load; highest accident-risk category.
📦 Dataset
🌏

Trained on the RDD2022 (Road Damage Dataset 2022) benchmark, a large-scale, multi-country dataset captured using smartphone cameras mounted in vehicles. The training subset used in this project comprises 1,530 annotated images from India and Japan, representing diverse road conditions, surface types, and lighting environments. Annotations include YOLO-format bounding boxes for four standardized damage classes (D00, D10, D20, D40) as defined by Japanese road inspection standards, ensuring global applicability and cross-domain generalizability of the trained model.
